# ADR-0016: Nutrition is coach-authored guidance, never generated prescription

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-18
- **Deciders:** Project author

## Context

"Diet recommendations" was requested as a platform feature. Nutrition is absent from
[problem.md](../problem.md) entirely — this is a new product surface, not a deferred one — so it
needs a decision before it needs a schema.

It is also the only requested feature that runs into an existing safety boundary. problem.md
already lists as a non-goal:

> Personalized medical, injury-rehab, or financial advice generated by the AI without instructor
> authority.

Nutrition sits uncomfortably close to that line, and the line is not academic:

- **Regulation is real and jurisdictional.** In much of the US, "medical nutrition therapy" is a
  licensed activity restricted to registered dietitians; several states restrict who may give
  individualised dietary advice at all. In the EU, software that diagnoses, prevents, monitors or
  treats a condition can qualify as a medical device (MDR Annex VIII); an app that adjusts intake
  for a diagnosed condition is in materially different territory from one that records meals.
- **Harm is plausible and specific.** Automated calorie targets are actively dangerous for people
  with eating disorders, for pregnant users, for diabetics on insulin, and for anyone with renal
  disease. These are not edge cases in a gym's membership.
- **The competitive advantage is not here.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) is explicit that the
  advantage is "the programme model, instructor workflow, adjustment engine, and longitudinal
  training data". A generated meal planner is a commodity feature that would import a large
  liability surface for no differentiation.

Meanwhile the legitimate need is real: coaches already give nutrition guidance to their clients,
today, in WhatsApp and PDFs. Refusing to model it at all does not stop it happening — it just
means it happens somewhere with no audit trail, no tenancy and no versioning.

## Decision

Model nutrition as **coach-authored guidance attached to a coaching relationship**, and refuse to
build automated dietary prescription.

**Will build:**

- `nutrition_guidance` — a document a coach writes for one athlete, versioned like a programme:
  authored, published, immutable once published, superseded rather than edited. Same reasoning as
  [ADR-0006](0006-immutable-program-versioning.md); "what my coach told me in March" must remain
  answerable.
- Optional structured targets a **coach** sets explicitly (protein, calories, hydration) —
  entered by a human who is accountable for them, attributed to that human, and shown as theirs.
- Member-side self-logging (meals, water, weight) if and when it earns its place. Recording what
  you ate is not advice.
- The AI may **retrieve and summarise** what a coach wrote ("what did Sam say about protein?").
  That is search over authored content, not generation.

**Will not build:**

- Automated or AI-generated calorie/macro targets, meal plans, or supplement recommendations.
- Anything keyed on a medical condition, diagnosis, medication, or pregnancy.
- Food-database-driven "nutrition scoring", or any output that would read as a clinical assessment.

**Guardrails on what we do build:**

- Nutrition guidance requires an **active coach–athlete relationship**. It is not a gym-wide
  broadcast, because dietary advice that is safe for one person is not safe for another.
- Every piece of guidance is **attributed to the coach who wrote it**, visibly, in the UI. The
  member must always know a person said this.
- Guidance carries a **disclaimer surface** distinguishing coaching guidance from medical advice,
  and the product will not present it as the latter.
- If the assistant is ever pointed at this data, it stays at **authority level 0–1**
  ([ADR-0007](0007-ai-authority-levels.md)) — retrieve and explain, never propose a change.
  Raising it needs a new ADR that supersedes this one, and a much better answer on liability than
  we have today.

## Alternatives considered

- **Full AI meal planning.** Highest apparent feature value, and what the request most directly
  implied. Rejected: it collides head-on with the existing AI non-goal, imports medical-device and
  scope-of-practice exposure across jurisdictions, and the failure mode is harm to a real person
  rather than a bad workout. If a gym wants this, they can employ a dietitian and use the
  coach-authored surface.
- **Integrate a third-party nutrition API** (food database + targets). Moves the liability only
  partly, adds a per-seat cost to a model whose economics assume no per-member fee
  ([cost-analysis.md](../cost-analysis.md)), and still surfaces generated targets under our brand.
- **Build nothing.** Honest, but coaches give this advice anyway; keeping it off-platform loses the
  audit trail and versioning that are the reason to be on-platform at all.
- **Defer entirely.** Effectively what happens — it is last in the build order
  ([feature-plan-2026-07.md](../feature-plan-2026-07.md) §10). This ADR exists so that when it is
  picked up, the boundary is already decided rather than argued at implementation time.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** a real coaching need is served; the liability surface stays close to what a human
  coach already carries; versioning and attribution make advice auditable; nothing in the AI
  authority model has to be relaxed.
- **Negative / costs:** less impressive than an automatic meal planner, and gyms will ask for one.
  That conversation is easier with this document than without it. Coaches must write guidance
  themselves, which is friction — deliberately, since the accountability is the point.
- **Follow-ups:** if a gym's dietitian is the author, consider whether a `dietitian` capacity is
  warranted rather than overloading `trainer`. Revisit only via a superseding ADR.
